# Light-Weight-Encryption - CTF Challenge Writeup

## The Vulnerability

This challenge implements a custom Learning With Errors (LWE) based encryption scheme.
The public key consists of a random matrix $A \in \mathbb{Z}_q^{m \times n}$ and a vector $B = A \cdot s + k \cdot e \pmod q$, where:
- $q = 2^{768}$
- $s$ is a secret vector in $\mathbb{Z}_q^n$
- $k = p \cdot sk^{-1} \pmod q$, where $p$ is a 520-bit prime and $sk$ is a 128-bit prime.
- $e$ is a small error vector where each component is bounded by a 32-bit prime $r$.

The critical flaw lies in the parameter sizes and the construction of $k$. Since $k \equiv p \cdot sk^{-1} \pmod q$, multiplying the entire equation by $sk$ gives:
$$sk \cdot B \equiv A \cdot (s \cdot sk) + p \cdot e \pmod q$$

Notice the bounds: $p \cdot e_i \approx 2^{520} \cdot 2^{32} = 2^{552}$. This error term is significantly smaller than the modulus $q = 2^{768}$. Because the error term $p \cdot e$ is strictly smaller than $q/2$, this equation holds exactly over the integers when formulated as a bounded error problem:
$$sk \cdot B - A \cdot s' - q \cdot y = p \cdot e$$
where $s' = s \cdot sk \pmod q$ and $y$ is an integer vector.

## The Solution

### Step 1: Primary Lattice Reduction to Recover $sk$
We construct a lattice using $q$, $A$, and $B$ to find $sk$ and the bounded error vector. By weighing $sk$ with a constant $W \approx 2^{424}$ (to match the length of $p \cdot e_i \approx 2^{552}$), we can run the LLL algorithm. To ensure the target vector is uniquely the shortest, we use a subset of $m_{sub} = 85$ equations, raising the shortest vector bound of the $(A, q)$ sub-lattice to $\approx 2^{623}$, which safely isolates our target vector of length $\approx 2^{553}$. The LLL algorithm successfully recovers $sk$.

### Step 2: Recovering the Full Error Vector
With $sk$ recovered, we can find a $16 \times 16$ invertible submatrix of $A$ modulo $q$ to solve for $s'$.
Once $s'$ is known, we can compute the true scaled errors $Z_i = p \cdot e_i$:
$$Z_i \equiv (B_i \cdot sk - A_i \cdot s') \pmod q$$
By normalizing $Z_i$ into the range $[-q/2, q/2]$, we recover $p \cdot e_i$ exactly over the integers.

### Step 3: Subset Sum Lattice to Recover $p$ and the Flag
The ciphertext is generated by selecting a subset $I$ of 130 indices from $A$ and $B$:
$$u = \sum_{i \in I} A_i \pmod q$$
$$v = pt - \sum_{i \in I} B_i \pmod q$$

Since $A_i$ are small integers, the sum $u = \sum_{i \in I} A_i$ does not wrap modulo $q$. This allows us to find valid subsets $x$ using a second LLL reduction (a classic integer knapsack/subset sum lattice).

Because the subset sum is not unique, we get a base solution $x_{base}$ and several null-space vectors $x_{null}$. 
We know that for any valid solution $x$, the difference in the error sum $\sum x_i Z_i - \sum I_i Z_i$ is a multiple of $p$. By taking the greatest common divisor of $\sum x_{null} \cdot Z$ across all null vectors, we beautifully extract the 520-bit prime $p$.

Finally, we substitute our base solution into the decryption equation:
$$Y = v \cdot sk + u \cdot s' \pmod q$$
$$pt \cdot sk \equiv Y + \sum x_{base} \cdot Z \pmod q$$
We can isolate $pt$ perfectly:
$$pt = \left((Y + \sum x_{base} \cdot Z \pmod q) \pmod p\right) \cdot sk^{-1} \pmod p$$
